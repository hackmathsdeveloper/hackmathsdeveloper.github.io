
A **minimal surface** is a surface that is a critical point of area: for every small deformation fixing its boundary, the first variation of area is zero. Equivalently, in Euclidean space its mean curvature vanishes identically. “Minimal” does not mean globally smallest-area; it means *locally stationary* for area, like an equilibrium soap film.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

## Core definition

For a smooth immersed surface \(X:\Sigma\to\mathbb R^3\), the mean-curvature vector is

\[
\vec H=\Delta_g X,
\]

where \(\Delta_g\) is the Laplace–Beltrami operator induced by the surface metric. The surface is minimal exactly when

\[
\vec H\equiv 0.
\]

This is the Euler–Lagrange equation for the area functional

\[
A(\Sigma)=\int_\Sigma dA.
\]

Thus minimal surfaces are the two-dimensional analogue of geodesics: geodesics are critical for length, while minimal surfaces are critical for area.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.7648v1)

A useful consequence is local saddle behavior: at a nonplanar point, a minimal surface curves upward in one normal direction and downward in the other. Its Gaussian curvature satisfies \(K\le 0\), with \(K=0\) only at planar points.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.13347v2)

## What “minimal” really means

If \(\Gamma\) is a closed curve, the **Plateau problem** asks for a surface of least area spanning \(\Gamma\). Any smooth interior solution is minimal. But a minimal surface need not minimize area globally: it may be only a saddle-point critical point, and it may have no boundary at all.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

For example:

- A flat plane is minimal.
- A small disk in the plane minimizes area among surfaces with the same circular boundary.
- The entire infinite plane has no boundary, so “global area minimization” is not even the right question.
- Many minimal surfaces, such as Enneper’s surface, are critical points but not global minimizers.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

## The main families

| Family | Defining feature | Canonical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Graphs | Given by \(z=u(x,y)\); minimality becomes the minimal-surface PDE | Plane, Scherk surfaces |
| Surfaces of revolution | Invariant under rotations about an axis | Plane, catenoid |
| Ruled surfaces | Swept out by straight lines | Plane, helicoid |
| Translating/periodic surfaces | Invariant under a translation or lattice | Riemann examples, Scherk towers, triply periodic surfaces |
| Finite-total-curvature surfaces | Complete with controlled ends; conformal data extend to a compact Riemann surface | Plane, catenoid, Costa–Hoffman–Meeks surfaces |
| Minimal surfaces in other ambients | Mean curvature vanishes in a curved Riemannian manifold | Great spheres in \(S^3\), totally geodesic surfaces, holomorphic curves in Kähler manifolds |

The plane, catenoid, and helicoid were for a long time essentially the only known complete embedded minimal surfaces of finite topology in \(\mathbb R^3\); modern theory revealed vastly richer families.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

## Classical examples

### Plane

The plane has zero mean curvature and zero Gaussian curvature. It is the simplest minimal surface and the only minimal surface that is both a graph over all of \(\mathbb R^2\) and flat.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

### Catenoid

The catenoid is the surface generated by rotating a catenary around an axis. It is the only nonplanar minimal surface of revolution, and physically it appears as the soap film between two parallel coaxial rings.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

### Helicoid

The helicoid resembles an infinite spiral ramp. It is the only nonplanar ruled minimal surface, and it is locally isometric to the catenoid: the two are conjugate minimal surfaces and can be deformed into one another through a one-parameter family of minimal surfaces.  [maths.cam.ac](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/features/less-more-quest-minimal-surfaces)

### Scherk surfaces

Scherk’s first surface is the graph

\[
z=\log\frac{\cos y}{\cos x},
\]

on a suitable domain. It was historically important as a nontrivial explicit solution of the minimal-surface equation and leads to singly, doubly, and triply periodic families.  [ugr](http://www.ugr.es/~fmartin/dibujos-clasicos.htm)

### Enneper surface

Enneper’s surface is a self-intersecting minimal immersion of the plane into \(\mathbb R^3\). It is a fundamental example because it has high symmetry, finite total curvature, and a particularly simple Weierstrass representation.  [arxiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05958.pdf)

### Costa–Hoffman–Meeks surfaces

Costa’s surface, proved embedded by Hoffman and Meeks, has genus one and three ends. The higher-genus \(M_k\) surfaces are complete, embedded, finite-total-curvature minimal surfaces with genus \(k\), two catenoidal ends, and one flat end. Their discovery transformed the classification landscape.  [ugr](http://www.ugr.es/~fmartin/dibujos-clasicos.htm)

## The analytic machinery

### Minimal-surface equation

If the surface is a graph \(z=u(x,y)\), minimality is the quasilinear elliptic PDE

\[
(1+u_y^2)u_{xx}-2u_xu_yu_{xy}+(1+u_x^2)u_{yy}=0.
\]

This is the **minimal-surface equation**. It is nonlinear, but elliptic, so minimal graph theory draws heavily on PDE methods: regularity, maximum principles, curvature estimates, and compactness.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12319v1.pdf)

### Weierstrass–Enneper representation

In \(\mathbb R^3\), every simply connected minimal surface can locally be produced from holomorphic data. One common form uses a holomorphic function \(f\) and a meromorphic function \(g\), with \(fg^2\) holomorphic:

\[
X(z)=\operatorname{Re}\int^z
\left(
\frac12 f(1-g^2),
\frac{i}{2}f(1+g^2),
fg
\right)\,dz.
\]

Here \(g\) is essentially the Gauss map and \(f\) controls the conformal metric. This turns minimal-surface construction into complex analysis: choose appropriate meromorphic data, integrate, and impose period conditions to obtain a well-defined surface.  [math.uchicago](https://math.uchicago.edu/~dannyc/courses/minimal_surfaces_2014/minimal_surfaces_notes.pdf)

The representation explains why minimal surfaces are so abundant: holomorphic functions and meromorphic maps provide an enormous supply of local examples, while global embeddedness, completeness, and end behavior impose difficult constraints.  [link.springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10711-019-00456-y?error=cookies_not_supported&code=77c40e7e-7008-4301-9b5d-42366d5965e0)

### Conjugate surfaces

Every minimal surface has an associated **conjugate minimal surface**, obtained by rotating the surface’s tangent plane by \(90^\circ\) in the normal direction. Conjugation preserves the intrinsic metric but changes extrinsic shape. The catenoid and helicoid are the classic conjugate pair.  [math.uci](https://www.math.uci.edu/~vmm/Surface/Minimal_Surfaces_2011_Matthias_Weber.pdf)

## Classification by topology and ends

For complete embedded minimal surfaces in \(\mathbb R^3\), major organizing invariants include:

- **Genus**: number of handles.
- **Number of ends**: asymptotic pieces going to infinity.
- **Total curvature**: \(\int_M K\,dA\).
- **Symmetry group**.
- **Periodicity**: none, singly, doubly, or triply periodic.

For finite-total-curvature examples, the surface is conformally a compact Riemann surface with finitely many punctures; its genus and end count become powerful classification coordinates. The plane has genus 0 and one end; the catenoid has genus 0 and two ends; Costa’s surface has genus 1 and three ends.  [projecteuclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/tohoku-mathematical-journal/volume-53/issue-4/Mean-curvature-1-surfaces-of-Costa-type-in-hyperbolic-three/10.2748/tmj/1113247804.pdf)

A landmark result is that properly embedded, complete, genus-zero minimal surfaces in \(\mathbb R^3\) are essentially Riemann’s minimal examples or their limiting cases, including the plane, catenoid, helicoid, and Scherk surfaces.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12319v1)

## Stability and global minimization

A minimal surface is **stable** if the second variation of area is nonnegative for all compactly supported variations. Stable surfaces are more than critical points: small perturbations cannot decrease area to second order.  [math.uchicago](https://math.uchicago.edu/~dannyc/courses/minimal_surfaces_2014/minimal_surfaces_notes.pdf)

This distinction matters:

- **Minimal**: first variation vanishes; \(H=0\).
- **Stable minimal**: second variation is nonnegative.
- **Area-minimizing**: area is no larger than that of every competitor with the same boundary.

Area-minimizing surfaces are stable, but stable minimal surfaces need not globally minimize area. In \(\mathbb R^3\), for instance, the catenoid is minimal but becomes unstable beyond a certain neck-size threshold.  [math.uchicago](https://math.uchicago.edu/~dannyc/courses/minimal_surfaces_2014/minimal_surfaces_notes.pdf)

## Minimal surfaces beyond \(\mathbb R^3\)

The definition generalizes immediately: a minimal submanifold of a Riemannian manifold \((M,g)\) has vanishing mean-curvature vector. In a curved ambient space, the ambient geometry changes both the examples and the classification.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06618v1)

Important settings include:

- **Spheres**: \(S^3\) contains many closed embedded minimal surfaces, unlike Euclidean \(\mathbb R^3\), where there are no compact boundaryless minimal surfaces.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22531v1)
- **Hyperbolic space**: asymptotic boundary data and curvature constraints play a central role.
- **Kähler manifolds**: complex curves are automatically minimal because their mean-curvature vector vanishes.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06618v1)
- **Product manifolds**: slices and products of geodesics give basic minimal examples, while curvature assumptions can forbid minimal spheres or force tori to be Lagrangian.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06618v1)

## Major theorems

- **Bernstein’s theorem**: an entire minimal graph over all of \(\mathbb R^2\) must be a plane. This is a striking global rigidity result.  [content-calpoly-edu.s3.amazonaws](https://content-calpoly-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/math/1/documents/polymath2010.pdf)
- **Douglas–Radó theorem**: every rectifiable Jordan curve in \(\mathbb R^3\) bounds a disk-type surface of least area, establishing existence for the Plateau problem.  [archive.lib.msu](https://archive.lib.msu.edu/crcmath/math/math/m/m251.htm)
- **Osserman–Gulliver regularity**: minimizing solutions do not develop interior singularities in the classical setting.  [archive.lib.msu](https://archive.lib.msu.edu/crcmath/math/math/m/m251.htm)
- **Meeks–Pérez–Ros-type classification**: properly embedded genus-zero minimal surfaces in \(\mathbb R^3\) are controlled by the Riemann minimal examples and their limits.  [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12319v1)
- **Min-max theory**: produces minimal surfaces from sweepouts of a manifold, providing existence results even in settings where direct minimization fails. This is central to modern differential geometry.

## Why they matter

Minimal surfaces sit at the intersection of:

- **Calculus of variations**: they solve the most basic area-extremization problem.
- **PDE theory**: their graph equation is a model nonlinear elliptic equation.
- **Complex analysis**: in \(\mathbb R^3\), the Weierstrass representation makes them objects built from holomorphic data.
- **Topology**: genus, ends, and fundamental group constrain possible examples.
- **Physics**: soap films model surface tension equilibrium; related variational surfaces occur in materials science, relativity, and biological modeling.  [pnas](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.0510379103)

In short, the “class of minimal surfaces” is not a single family but a vast geometric universe: locally saddle-shaped, mean-curvature-zero surfaces governed by elliptic PDE, generated locally by complex-analytic data, and classified globally by subtle interactions among topology, ends, symmetry, stability, and ambient curvature.  [math.uchicago](https://math.uchicago.edu/~dannyc/courses/minimal_surfaces_2014/minimal_surfaces_notes.pdf)
