● Here are 10 olympiad-style geometry problems, roughly ordered from accessible
  to hard:

  1. Angle chasing (entry level). In triangle $ABC$, the incircle touches $BC$,
     $CA$, $AB$ at $D$, $E$, $F$ respectively. If $\angle A = 60°$, find $\angle
     EDF$.
  2. Power of a point. Two circles intersect at $P$ and $Q$. A line through $P$
     meets the circles again at $A$ and $B$. Prove that the tangents to the
     circles at $A$ and $B$ meet on the line through $Q$ perpendicular to $AB$.
     (Variant: prove $\frac{AQ}{BQ}$ is constant as the line rotates.)
  3. Cyclic quadrilaterals. Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral with diagonals
     meeting at $E$. Prove that
     $$\frac{AE}{CE} = \frac{AB \cdot AD}{CB \cdot CD}.$$
  4. Nine-point circle / Euler line. In acute triangle $ABC$ with orthocenter
     $H$ and circumcenter $O$, let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Prove that $AH =
     2 \cdot OM$.
  5. Simson line. Point $P$ lies on the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$. Prove
     that the feet of the perpendiculars from $P$ to the three sides are
     collinear.
  6. Homothety. Two disjoint circles are tangent externally at $T$. A common
     external tangent touches them at $A$ and $B$. Prove that $\angle ATB =
     90°$.
  7. Butterfly theorem. Let $M$ be the midpoint of chord $PQ$ of a circle. Two
     other chords $AB$ and $CD$ pass through $M$, with $AD$ and $BC$ meeting
     $PQ$ at $X$ and $Y$. Prove that $M$ is also the midpoint of $XY$.
  8. Mixed-linear / A-mixtilinear incircle. The $A$-mixtilinear incircle of
     triangle $ABC$ (tangent to sides $AB$, $AC$ and internally tangent to the
     circumcircle) touches the circumcircle at $T$. Prove that line $AT$ passes
     through the tangency point of the incircle with $BC$. (IMO 1978 flavor; 
     requires inversion or homothety.)
  9. Isogonal conjugates. Point $P$ is inside acute triangle $ABC$. Let $\ell_a$
     be the reflection of line $AP$ across the angle bisector of $\angle A$,
     and define $\ell_b$, $\ell_c$ similarly. Prove that $\ell_a$, $\ell_b$,
     $\ell_c$ are concurrent.
  10. IMO 2000/1 (hard). Two circles $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ intersect at $M$
      and $N$. A common tangent touches $\Gamma_1$ at $P$ and $\Gamma_2$ at $Q$,
      with $M$ closer to line $PQ$ than $N$. Line $PN$ meets $\Gamma_2$ again
      at $R$. Prove that $MQ$ bisects $\angle PMR$.
