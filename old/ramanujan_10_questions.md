To emulate the mathematical style found in Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook, one must focus on **identities recorded without proof**, often involving **theta functions, continued fractions, modular equations, and partition statistics**. Ramanujan’s compositions frequently highlight the **"supreme secret" of elegant mathematical relations** discovered through deep empirical intuition and laborious calculation.

Below are 10 questions styled after Ramanujan’s compositions, followed by the explorations they make possible.

### 1. Partition Congruences and the Crank
**Question:** If $F_a(q) = \frac{(q;q)_\infty}{(aq;q)_\infty(q/a;q)_\infty} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \lambda_n q^n$, for what primes $p$ does a $p$-dissection exist such that $\lambda_{pn+r} \equiv 0 \pmod{\Phi_p(a)}$?.
*   **Possible Explorations:** This allows for the **combinatorial explanation of Ramanujan’s famous congruences** for $p(n)$. One can explore **crank and rank statistics** to partition the set of partitions into equinumerous classes, providing a visual and structural understanding of why certain numbers of partitions are divisible by 5, 7, or 11.

### 2. Reciprocity of Continued Fractions
**Question:** Let $R(q)$ be the Rogers-Ramanujan continued fraction. If $\alpha\beta = \pi^2$, show that $(\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2} + R(e^{-2\alpha}))(\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2} + R(e^{-2\beta})) = \frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** This leads to the **evaluation of continued fractions in finite terms** for rational values of $n$ when $x=e^{-\pi\sqrt{n}}$. Such "reciprocity theorems" allow researchers to link values at one argument to another, facilitating the **calculation of singular moduli and class invariants**.

### 3. Differential Equations of Eisenstein Series
**Question:** Define $P, Q, R$ as the Eisenstein series of weights 2, 4, and 6. Show that $q \frac{dP}{dq} = \frac{P^2-Q}{12}$ and determine similar expressions for $Q$ and $R$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** These identities provide the foundation for **nonlinear differential equations** that relate modular forms. Explorations include finding **series representations for $1/\pi$** and proving congruences for the **Ramanujan tau-function $\tau(n)$**.

### 4. Double Series of Bessel Functions
**Question:** Let $F(x) = [x]$ if $x \notin \mathbb{Z}$ and $x - 1/2$ if $x \in \mathbb{Z}$. Prove that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} F(\frac{x}{n}) = \pi x + \sqrt{x} \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sqrt{\frac{n}{m}} J_1(4\pi\sqrt{mnx})$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** These identities are intimately connected to the **classical circle and Dirichlet divisor problems**. They allow for the study of **conditional convergence in double series** and the development of **weighted divisor sums** to estimate error terms in lattice point problems.

### 5. Identities for the Rogers-Ramanujan Functions
**Question:** Given $G(q)$ and $H(q)$, show that $H(q)G(q^{11}) - q^2 G(q)H(q^{11}) = 1$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** Ramanujan compiled a list of **forty such identities**. Exploring these leads to **modular equations of various degrees** (e.g., 5, 7, 11) and provides insights into the **theory of mock theta functions** and their asymptotic expansions as $q \to 1^-$.

### 6. Radical Identities and Componendo et Dividendo
**Question:** If $g^4 = 5$, prove that $\frac{\sqrt{3+2g} - \sqrt{4-4g}}{\sqrt{3+2g} + \sqrt{4-4g}} = 2+g+g^2+g^3$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** This involves the algebraic **simplification of nested radicals** and the application of the principle of **componendo et dividendo**. It prompts explorations into how such **elegant algebraic oddities** can be derived from minimal auxiliary conditions.

### 7. Highly Composite Numbers and Representation by Squares
**Question:** Let $Q_{2k}(N)$ denote the number of ways to represent $N$ as a sum of $2k$ squares. Find the maximal order of $Q_{2k}(N)$ as $N \to \infty$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** This extends Ramanujan's study of **highly composite numbers** to other arithmetic functions. One can explore **the maximal order of divisor functions $\sigma_{-s}(N)$** under the **Riemann Hypothesis**, as well as the behavior of largely composite numbers.

### 8. Partial Theta Function Transformations
**Question:** Prove that $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{q^n}{(q;q)_n^2} = \frac{1}{(q;q)_\infty^2} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n q^{n(n+1)/2}$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** These "partial theta functions" consist of terms with positive indices from Jacobi theta functions. Explorations include **Warnaar’s theory**, the study of **false theta functions**, and finding **combinatorial bijections** for generating functions of gradual stacks with summits.

### 9. Asymptotic Expansion of Gamma Function Zeroes
**Question:** Let $q_0(a)$ be the least positive zero of $1 - \frac{aq}{1} - \frac{aq^2}{1} - \dots = 0$. Show that $q_0 \sim \frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{a^2} + \frac{2}{a^3} - \frac{6}{a^4} + \dots$ as $a \to \infty$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** This facilitates the **numerical approximation of zeros** of continued fractions, which is useful in studying **birth and death processes** and the enumeration of "coins in a fountain". It allows for the comparison of **different methods of successive approximation**.

### 10. Integral Analogues of Gauss Sums
**Question:** Define $\phi_w(t) = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{cos(\pi tx)}{cosh(\pi x)} e^{-\pi wx^2} dx$. Determine the quasiperiodic relation for $\phi_w(t+2i)$.
*   **Possible Explorations:** These integrals function as **continuous analogues of theta functions**. They allow for the exploration of **transformation formulas** and the construction of **character analogues of Gauss sums**, which have applications in evaluating Dirichlet $L$-series.
