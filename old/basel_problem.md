Here are 20 **different variations** of the Basel Problem, ranging from direct generalizations to closely related series and problem styles. The classical Basel Problem is to evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}\), which Euler showed equals \(\frac{\pi^2}{6}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)

## Direct variants

1. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n)^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
2. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n-1)^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
3. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
4. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^4}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
5. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^6}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
6. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2k}}\) for a positive integer \(k\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)

## Restricted-index variants

7. Evaluate \(\sum_{p\ \text{prime}}\frac{1}{p^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
8. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(an+b)^2}\) for fixed integers \(a,b\) with no zero denominator. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
9. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T_n^2}\), where \(T_n=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\) is the \(n\)-th triangular number. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
10. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F_n^2}\), where \(F_n\) is the \(n\)-th Fibonacci number, or determine whether it has a simple closed form. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)

## Harmonic-mixed variants

11. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_n}{n^2}\), where \(H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{k}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
12. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_n^{(2)}}{n^2}\), where \(H_n^{(2)}=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{k^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
13. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_n}{n^3}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
14. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}H_n}{n^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)

## Function-based variants

15. Prove \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}\) using the infinite product for \(\sin x\). [math.cmu](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~bwsulliv/basel-problem.pdf)
16. Prove the same identity using Fourier series or Parseval’s theorem. [math.cmu](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~bwsulliv/basel-problem.pdf)
17. Derive \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}\) from the Fourier expansion of \(f(x)=x\) or \(f(x)=x^2\) on a suitable interval. [math.cmu](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~bwsulliv/basel-problem.pdf)
18. Evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2+x^2}\) for real \(x>0\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)

## Parameter and multidimensional variants

19. Study \(S(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^s}\) and evaluate it at \(s=2\), then compare with nearby values such as \(s=3\) and \(s=4\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
20. Evaluate the double sum \(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(m+n)^2}\), or determine whether it converges. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)

## More polished problem statements

If you want them in a more contest-style format, here are 5 example rewrites:

- Show that the sum of the reciprocals of the odd squares is a rational multiple of \(\pi^2\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
- Find a closed form for \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^2}\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
- Use Parseval’s identity to evaluate \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}\). [math.cmu](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~bwsulliv/basel-problem.pdf)
- Generalize Euler’s result by finding \(\zeta(4)\) and \(\zeta(6)\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
- Investigate whether sums of reciprocal squares over special subsets, such as primes or triangular numbers, admit closed forms. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_problem)
