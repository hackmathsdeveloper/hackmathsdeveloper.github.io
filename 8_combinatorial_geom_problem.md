Here are 8 concrete combinatorial geometry problem templates (competition‑style) drawn from the categories I listed, phrased generically so you can instantiate them with parameters as you like:

## Point–line incidences

1. Let \(P\) be a set of \(n\) points and \(L\) a set of \(m\) lines in the plane. Show that the number of incidences \(I(P,L)\) (pairs \((p,\ell)\) with \(p\in P, \ell\in L, p\in\ell\)) satisfies an upper bound of the form  
   \[
   I(P,L) \le C \bigl(n^{2/3} m^{2/3} + n + m\bigr)
   \]  
   for some absolute constant \(C\). [web.mat.upc](https://web.mat.upc.edu/juan.jose.rue/BlockCourse-FUBerlin-Week1/Berlin_Handout4.pdf)

## Erdős–Szekeres / convex polygon

2. For a given integer \(k\ge 3\), determine the least integer \(N(k)\) such that any set of \(N(k)\) points in the plane in general position (no three collinear) contains \(k\) points that form the vertices of a convex \(k\)-gon. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93Szekeres_conjecture)

3. Show that any set of 5 points in the plane in general position contains 4 points that form a convex quadrilateral. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93Szekeres_conjecture)

## Helly / convex sets

4. Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be a finite family of convex subsets of \(\mathbb{R}^2\). Suppose every triple of sets in \(\mathcal{F}\) has nonempty intersection. Prove that the whole family has nonempty intersection. [artofproblemsolving](https://artofproblemsolving.com/community/c1285h1012490_combinatorial_geometry)

5. In \(\mathbb{R}^d\), let \(\mathcal{F}\) be a family of convex sets such that any \(d+1\) of them intersect. Show that all sets in \(\mathcal{F}\) have a point in common (Helly’s theorem in \(\mathbb{R}^d\)). [ipam.ucla](https://www.ipam.ucla.edu/programs/workshops/workshop-ii-combinatorial-geometry/)

## Covering / packing

6. Given a convex body \(K\subset\mathbb{R}^2\), find (or bound) the minimum number of translates of \(K\) required to cover another fixed convex region \(R\), in terms of area or other parameters of \(K\) and \(R\). [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/abs/pii/B9780720422627500296)

7. Given \(n\) congruent disks of radius 1 in the plane, determine (or bound) the maximum possible number of pairwise disjoint disks that can be placed inside a fixed convex region (e.g., a square of side \(L\)), and the minimum number of disks needed to cover it. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/abs/pii/B9780720422627500296)

## Arrangements / partitions

8. Let \(n\) lines in general position in the plane (no two parallel, no three concurrent) be given.  
   a) Show that they divide the plane into \(\frac{n^2+n+2}{2}\) regions.  
   b) For such lines, determine the maximum possible number of regions that are unbounded. [static1.squarespace](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5fe101b108d85d5e817a934a/t/60e5a4b082270f3b7f196323/1625662641520/Points_and_Lines_in_Combinatorial_Geometry_Katedralskolan_Lund.pdf)
