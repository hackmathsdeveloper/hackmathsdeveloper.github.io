
Here are **20 distinct pattern variations** you can develop from Viète’s formula. They range from direct mathematical extensions to visual, computational, historical, and cross-disciplinary treatments.

Viète’s product is naturally expressed as

\[
\frac{2}{\pi}
=
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{n+1}}\right)
=
\frac{\sqrt2}{2}
\cdot
\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}
\cdot
\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}}{2}
\cdots,
\]

with the nested radicals arising from repeated use of the cosine half-angle identity. The geometry can be read through successive inscribed regular polygons whose numbers of sides double. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C3%A8te's_formula)

## 1. Geometric derivation patterns

1. **Inscribed-polygon area telescope**  
   Start with a unit circle and compare the areas of inscribed \(4\)-, \(8\)-, \(16\)-, \(32\)-gons, and so on. Show that each area ratio becomes one cosine factor:
   \[
   \frac{A_{2n}}{A_n}=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)^{-1}
   \]
   in an appropriate indexing scheme, then telescope the finite product toward the area \(\pi\) of the circle.

2. **Inscribed-polygon perimeter telescope**  
   Replace areas with perimeters. Let
   \[
   P_N=2N\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{N}\right)
   \]
   for the perimeter of a regular \(N\)-gon inscribed in the unit circle. Doubling \(N\) produces the same cosine-product structure.

3. **Chord-bisection construction**  
   Treat every radical as a chord length generated by bisecting a central angle. Construct the sequence geometrically with compass-and-straightedge logic:
   \[
   90^\circ,\ 45^\circ,\ 22.5^\circ,\ 11.25^\circ,\ldots
   \]

4. **Apothem-and-triangle decomposition**  
   Decompose a regular \(2^n\)-gon into congruent isosceles triangles. Use
   \[
   A_n=\frac12 P_n a_n,
   \]
   where \(a_n\) is the apothem, and show that angle bisection drives the recurrence.

5. **Archimedean bounds variation**  
   Pair Viète’s inscribed polygons with circumscribed polygons to create lower and upper bounds:
   \[
   P_n^{\mathrm{in}} < 2\pi < P_n^{\mathrm{out}}.
   \]
   This turns the product into a convergence-and-error-bound exploration rather than only an identity.

## 2. Trigonometric derivation patterns

6. **Repeated double-angle factorization**  
   Begin with
   \[
   \sin x=2\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{x}{2}\right),
   \]
   iterate it \(n\) times, and obtain
   \[
   \sin x
   =
   2^n\sin\left(\frac{x}{2^n}\right)
   \prod_{k=1}^n
   \cos\left(\frac{x}{2^k}\right).
   \]
   Set \(x=\pi/2\), then take \(n\to\infty\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C3%A8te's_formula)

7. **Sinc-function limit derivation**  
   Rearrange the finite identity into
   \[
   \prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\left(\frac{x}{2^k}\right)
   =
   \frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot
   \frac{x}{2^n\sin(x/2^n)}.
   \]
   Since
   \[
   \lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1,
   \]
   derive the general product
   \[
   \frac{\sin x}{x}
   =
   \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\cos\left(\frac{x}{2^k}\right).
   \]

8. **Cosine half-angle recursion**  
   Define
   \[
   c_0=0,\qquad
   c_{n+1}=\sqrt{\frac{1+c_n}{2}}.
   \]
   Then prove
   \[
   c_n=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{n+1}}\right).
   \]
   This creates a recurrence-first route to Viète’s radicals.

9. **Sine half-angle variant**  
   Instead use
   \[
   \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
   =
   \sqrt{\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}}.
   \]
   The signs and initial conditions change, but the same polygon-doubling machinery appears. This is useful for contrasting why Viète’s classical expression naturally favors cosine.

10. **Tangent product companion**  
    Use
    \[
    \tan x
    =
    2^n\tan\left(\frac{x}{2^n}\right)
    \prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\left(\frac{x}{2^k}\right).
    \]
    Taking a limit gives another route to the product and connects the construction to circumscribed polygons, since tangent governs their side lengths.

## 3. Algebraic and radical patterns

11. **Recursive nested-radical sequence**  
    Define
    \[
    r_1=\sqrt2,\qquad r_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+r_n}.
    \]
    Then establish
    \[
    r_n=2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{n+1}}\right),
    \]
    so that the \(n\)-term Viète approximation is
    \[
    V_n=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{r_k}{2}.
    \]

12. **Finite-product closed form**  
    Instead of treating the expression as inherently infinite, derive the exact finite formula:
    \[
    \prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{k+1}}\right)
    =
    \frac{1}{2^n\sin\left(\pi/2^{n+1}\right)}.
    \]
    This makes numerical calculation and error analysis cleaner.

13. **Chebyshev-polynomial viewpoint**  
    Since
    \[
    T_{2^n}\!\left(\cos\theta\right)=\cos(2^n\theta),
    \]
    set \(\theta=\pi/2^{n+1}\). The radical expressions become algebraic solutions generated by repeated quadratic extensions.

14. **Constructible-number interpretation**  
    Explain why every finite-stage radical is constructible: the successive angle halvings
    \[
    \frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{8},\frac{\pi}{16},\ldots
    \]
    correspond to regular polygons with \(2^m\) sides, all constructible by repeated square-root operations.

15. **Minimal-polynomial ladder**  
    Compute the minimal polynomials of values such as
    \[
    2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}
    \]
    and
    \[
    2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{16}\right)
    =
    \sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}.
    \]
    This leads to cyclotomic fields and the algebra of regular \(2^n\)-gons.

## 4. Numerical and algorithmic patterns

16. **Partial-product convergence table**  
    Compute
    \[
    \pi_n=\frac{2}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos(\pi/2^{k+1})}
    \]
    for \(n=1,2,\ldots,20\), then tabulate decimal digits correct. This shows that the product converges, but relatively slowly compared with modern \(\pi\)-algorithms.

17. **Error asymptotics**  
    Use the expansion
    \[
    \log(\cos t)=-\frac{t^2}{2}+O(t^4)
    \]
    to estimate the tail error:
    \[
    \log\left(
    \prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}
    \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{k+1}}\right)
    \right)
    \approx
    -\frac{\pi^2}{24\cdot4^n}.
    \]
    This reveals essentially geometric convergence with ratio near \(1/4\) in the asymptotic error.

18. **Stable numerical implementation**  
    Compare two computational strategies:
    \[
    r_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+r_n}
    \]
    versus direct evaluation of \(\cos(\pi/2^{n+1})\). Study floating-point rounding, underflow avoidance, and whether accumulating \(\log V_n\) improves robustness for very large products.

19. **Arbitrary-precision benchmark**  
    Implement Viète’s product in Python, Julia, C++, or arbitrary-precision libraries such as MPFR/GMP. Compare digits-per-operation against:
    - Leibniz’s alternating series,
    - Machin-like arctangent formulas,
    - Gauss–Legendre,
    - Borwein-type algorithms,
    - Chudnovsky’s series.

20. **Animation or interactive visualization**  
    Create a browser or Python visualization that doubles polygon sides at every iteration:
    \[
    4\to8\to16\to32\to\cdots.
    \]
    At each step display the polygon, its perimeter or area, the newly introduced cosine factor, its nested radical, the current approximation to \(\pi\), and the remaining error. This makes the limiting process concrete rather than merely symbolic.

## One unified project pattern

A particularly rich variation is a **“geometry \(\to\) trigonometry \(\to\) algebra \(\to\) numerical analysis”** presentation:

1. Inscribe a square in the unit circle.
2. Repeatedly double the number of sides.
3. Derive the area or perimeter ratio between successive polygons.
4. Identify the ratio as a cosine of a half-angle.
5. Convert the cosines into nested radicals.
6. Form the finite product.
7. Take its limit to obtain \(2/\pi\).
8. Measure the approximation error after each doubling.

That structure mirrors the historical geometric intuition while also revealing the modern analytic identity

\[
\frac{\sin x}{x}
=
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\cos\left(\frac{x}{2^n}\right),
\]

of which Viète’s product follows at \(x=\pi/2\). [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C3%A8te's_formula)
