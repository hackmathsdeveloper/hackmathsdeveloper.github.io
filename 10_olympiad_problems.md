
Here are 10 hard olympiad‑style problems across algebra, NT, combinatorics, and geometry. They’re original combinations of standard themes and are at roughly “shortlist / strong national olympiad” level.

***

### Problem 1 (Functional inequality, Cauchy/Jensen flavor)

Find all functions \(f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) such that for all real \(x,y\),
\[
f(x+y) + f(x-y) = 2f(x) + 2f(y)
\]
and
\[
f(x^2) \ge f(x)^2.
\]

***

### Problem 2 (Diophantine / exponential)

Determine all pairs of positive integers \((m,n)\) such that
\[
2^m - 3^n = 1.
\]

***

### Problem 3 (Invariant / extremal combinatorics on a grid)

On an \(n\times n\) chessboard, each cell contains an integer.  
You may repeatedly perform the following operation:

- Choose a row or column and add 1 to every entry in it.

Initially, the sum of all entries is 0.  
Prove that there exists some \(n_0\) such that for all integers \(n \ge n_0\), it is impossible to reach a position where all entries are distinct.

***

### Problem 4 (Graph theory / parity)

Let \(n\ge 3\) be an odd integer.  
Consider a simple graph \(G\) on \(2n\) vertices such that:

- Every vertex has degree at least \(n\).
- The graph is triangle‑free.

Prove that \(G\) is bipartite.

***

### Problem 5 (Inequality with symmetric constraint)

Let \(a,b,c>0\) and \(a+b+c=3\).  
Prove that
\[
\frac{a^2}{b+c} + \frac{b^2}{c+a} + \frac{c^2}{a+b} \ge \frac{3}{2} (a+b+c) - 1.
\]

***

### Problem 6 (Number theory, multiplicative structure)

Let \(p\) be an odd prime.  
Suppose that the integers \(1,2,\dots,p-1\) can be colored red or blue such that for any red integers \(a,b\), the product \(ab \bmod p\) is also red.  
Show that all nonzero residues modulo \(p\) have the same color.

***

### Problem 7 (Combinatorial geometry – convex position)

Let \(n\ge 5\).  
Let \(S\) be a set of \(2n-3\) points in the plane, no three of which are collinear.  
Prove that \(S\) contains \(n\) points that are the vertices of a convex \(n\)-gon.

(This is a sharpened Erdős–Szekeres–type statement when you compare to the classical bounds.) [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93Szekeres_conjecture)

***

### Problem 8 (Inequality on reals with symmetry)

Let \(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n\) be real numbers such that \(x_1+\cdots+x_n = 0\) and \(x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2 = 1\).  
Show that
\[
\sum_{1\le i<j\le n} \frac{1}{1 + x_i x_j} \ge \frac{n(n-1)}{2}.
\]

***

### Problem 9 (Sequences and combinatorics)

A sequence \((a_k)_{k\ge 1}\) of positive integers satisfies:

- \(a_1 = 1\),  
- for all \(k\ge 1\), \(a_{k+1}\) is divisible by \(a_k\),  
- for any positive integer \(m\), there is at most one index \(k\) such that \(a_k = m\).

Prove that there are infinitely many integers \(n\) such that
\[
a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n
\]
is a perfect square.

***

### Problem 10 (Functional equation with injectivity flavor)

Find all functions \(f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) such that for all real \(x,y\),
\[
f(x+f(y)) = f(x) + y.
\]
