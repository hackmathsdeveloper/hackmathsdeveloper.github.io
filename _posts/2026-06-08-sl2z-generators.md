## 1. The Two Generators

The standard generators for $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ are:

$$
S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

These matrices generate the entire group $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Verification:**
- $\det(S) = 0 \cdot 0 - (-1) \cdot 1 = 1$ ✓
- $\det(T) = 1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0 = 1$ ✓
- Both have integer entries ✓

---

## 2. Key Properties

**Important Relations:**

- $S^2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\\\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -I$
- $S^4 = I$ (order 4 in $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$)
- $T^n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & n \\\\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ for any $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $(ST)^3 = -I$ or equivalently $(ST)^6 = I$

The group has the presentation:

$$
SL_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \langle S, T \mid S^4 = I, (ST)^6 = I, S^2(ST)^3 = I \rangle.
$$

## 3. Example Matrices Generated by S and T

Here are some examples of matrices in $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ expressed as products of $S$ and $T$:

1. **Identity:** $I = S^4 = T^0$

2. **$S^2 = -I$:** $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\\\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$

3. **$T^2$:** $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\\\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

4. **$ST$:** $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\\\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

5. **$TS$:** $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\\\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

6. **$STS$:** $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\\\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (a lower-triangular matrix with 1s on the diagonal)

7. **$T^{-1}$:** $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\\\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## 4. The General Picture

Any matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\\\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ can be written as a product:

$$
M = \pm T^{a_1} S T^{a_2} S \cdots T^{a_k} S T^{a_{k+1}},
$$

where the $a_i$ come from the **continued fraction expansion** of $a/c$ (when $c \neq 0$).

This connection between $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ generators and continued fractions is deep: it is the same mechanism by which the Euclidean algorithm terminates, and it explains why $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acts naturally on the extended rational numbers via Möbius transformations.

---

## 5. Geometric Interpretation

$SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acts on the upper half-plane $\mathbb{H} = \lbrace z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Im}(z) \gt 0\rbrace$ by Möbius transformations:

$$
\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \cdot z = \frac{az + b}{cz + d}.
$$

Under this action:
- $T: z \mapsto z + 1$ is a horizontal translation by 1.
- $S: z \mapsto -1/z$ is an inversion through the unit circle followed by reflection.

The region $\lbrace z \in \mathbb{H} : |z| \ge 1, |\operatorname{Re}(z)| \le 1/2\rbrace$ is a fundamental domain — every point in $\mathbb{H}$ can be mapped into it by repeated application of $S$ and $T$. This geometric fact proves that $S$ and $T$ generate the group.

---

## 6. Deeper Significance

The generators $S$ and $T$ are not just convenient — they are fundamental. Every modular form $f$ satisfies:

$$
f(z+1) = f(z) \quad \text{($T$-invariance)}, \qquad f(-1/z) = z^k f(z) \quad \text{($S$-covariance)}.
$$

So checking modularity reduces to checking just these two transformations! The fact that $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is generated by $S$ and $T$ means the entire infinite-dimensional theory of modular forms is controlled by two functional equations.
