Equations and their characteristics

Convention used here: p counts turns around the small tube (meridional turns), and q counts revolutions around the central axis (longitudinal turns). Some references swap these labels. This convention produces the p-lobed overhead views in the supplied image.

x(t) = (R + r cos(pt)) cos(qt)
y(t) = (R + r cos(pt)) sin(qt)
z(t) = r sin(pt),   0 ≤ t ≤ 2π,   R > r > 0

The curve lies exactly on the ring torus:

(√(x² + y²) − R)² + z² = r²
  • Integer winding: p and q synchronize two circular motions. The centerline is periodic and smooth.
  • Coprimality: gcd(p,q) = 1 gives one embedded closed curve. With d = gcd(p,q) > 1, the full torus link has d components. Simply tracing the displayed formula for noncoprime p,q retraces one component d times; this application explicitly generates all d components.
  • Topology versus geometry: R and r change the shape, not the knot type while R > r > 0. Swapping p and q gives an equivalent knot, though generally a different geometric embedding. Reflecting z produces its mirror.
  • Minimal crossings: c = min((p−1)q, (q−1)p) for positive p,q. The number of crossings visible from a particular camera may be larger.
  • Knot genus: g = (p−1)(q−1)/2 when p,q are coprime. This is the minimum genus of an orientable spanning surface, not the genus of the supporting torus, which is always 1.
  • Degenerate and limiting cases: p = 1 or q = 1 yields an unknot. Noninteger frequencies need not close after 2π; an irrational ratio of angular frequencies gives a nonclosing trajectory dense on the torus.

For a link, component k uses u = (p/d)t + 2πk/q and v = (q/d)t, with k = 0,…,d−1; set x = (R+r cos u) cos v, y = (R+r cos u) sin v, z = r sin u. These are the distinct solutions of q u − p v = 0 modulo 2π.
