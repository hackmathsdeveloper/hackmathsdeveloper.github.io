
To get to IMO Shortlist level, you want (1) the right material, and (2) a deliberate training loop around it. Here’s a focused roadmap with concrete resources.

## 1. Core problem sources (Shortlist-grade)

These are your main “truth data” for what Shortlist difficulty actually is.

- Official IMO Shortlists (2005–present):  
  PDFs with all problems and official solutions, freely available from the IMO site. [imo-official](https://www.imo-official.org/assets/documents/problems/2024/IMO2024SL.pdf)
  Start around 2000+; the style matches modern contests.

- IMO Compendium (Djukić–Jukić–Mathematical Olympiad community):  
  Massive curated collection of all IMO and Shortlist problems, organized by topic; excellent for targeted drilling. [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)

- IMO Problems and Solutions on AoPS:  
  Indexed problems with community solutions and discussion; useful to see multiple approaches and “standard tricks”. [artofproblemsolving](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/IMO_Problems_and_Solutions)

- Experienced contestants’ Shortlist writeups (e.g., Anzo Teh’s solutions):  
  Contains full solutions and commentary to many recent Shortlists; good to study how a medalist actually thinks. [anzoteh96.wordpress](https://anzoteh96.wordpress.com/maths/)

## 2. Topic-deepening books at Shortlist level

These are not “intro” olympiad books; they are aimed squarely at advanced contestants.

**All topics / mixed**

- Engel – *Problem-Solving Strategies* (hard problem sets, deep technique coverage). [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
- Andreescu & Gelca – *Mathematical Olympiad Challenges*. [cambomaths.wordpress](https://cambomaths.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/03/mathematical-olympiad-challenges-2nd-edition-feb-2009.pdf)
- Andreescu & Dospinescu – *Problems from the Book* and *Straight from the Book* (elegant problems, many taken from Shortlists). [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)

**Algebra / inequalities / polynomials**

- Yufei Zhao’s handouts (inequalities, integer polynomials, advanced polynomial techniques). [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
- Various inequality / polynomial sections in Engel and the Compendium. [mathematicalolympiads.wordpress](https://mathematicalolympiads.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/imo-compendium.pdf)

**Number theory**

- Yufei Zhao – NT handouts (e.g., \(a^n\pm 1\), LTE, multiplicative functions). [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
- Russian-style problem books via Compendium references & Andreescu’s NT-focused texts. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/457705664/How-did-you-win-a-medal-in-the-IMO)

**Combinatorics**

- Zhao – Algebraic techniques in combinatorics, double counting/linear algebra handouts. [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
- Engel’s combinatorics chapters. [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)

**Geometry**

- Zhao – *Cyclic Quadrilaterals — The Big Picture*, *Three Lemmas in Geometry*, *Circles*, etc. [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
- National training notes (e.g., Anzo’s “Geometry big guns”, “Geometry theorems”). [anzoteh96.wordpress](https://anzoteh96.wordpress.com/maths/)

## 3. High-yield handouts and blogs

These bridge contest problems with “how to think” at Shortlist level.

- Yufei Zhao’s Olympiad training page: topic-wise handouts in roughly increasing difficulty, with many Shortlist-grade examples. [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
- Evan Chen’s olympiad articles: expository notes, problem collections, and commentary on tests that cover many Shortlist problems. [web.evanchen](https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html)
- Anzo Teh’s training notes and Shortlist solution archives for 2012–2016 (and later): shows solutions and thought processes close to actual training. [anzoteh96.wordpress](https://anzoteh96.wordpress.com/maths/)
- General advice and resource lists on Reddit / blogs from ex-IMO contestants, highlighting Shortlist and Longlist as primary training sources. [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/math/comments/cdy7mn/does_anyone_have_resources_to_study_for_the_imo/)

## 4. Training methodology (how to actually “master” Shortlist)

Given your background, treat this like a deliberate research pipeline rather than just “doing problems”:

1. **Baseline and topic gaps (2–3 weeks)**  
   - Solve 10–15 past IMO P1/P4 across years without time limits.  
   - Solve 5–10 “easy” Shortlist problems per area (A/C/G/NT), picking from the Compendium filters or Shortlists with difficulty 1–2. [artofproblemsolving](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/IMO_Problems_and_Solutions)
   - For each problem, classify: “I could solve with standard tools” vs “needed a new trick”.

2. **Topic blocks (6–12 weeks per cycle)**  
   Run 2–3 topic blocks in parallel, e.g. Geometry + NT + Combinatorics.

   For each topic block:
   - Choose 1–2 deep references (e.g. Zhao geometry handouts + Engel). [yufeizhao](https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/)
   - Pick 20–30 Shortlist problems in that area, stratified by year and difficulty (use Compendium and official Shortlists). [imo-official](https://www.imo-official.org/assets/documents/problems/2024/IMO2024SL.pdf)
   - For each problem:
     - Spend ~60–90 minutes before peeking at hints or solutions (simulate exam conditions).  
     - After solving or giving up, analyze the official and 1–2 alternative solutions (AoPS / blogs) and extract **named techniques** and **config patterns**, writing them into your own “tech notes”. [web.evanchen](https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html)

3. **Timed sets and simulation (ongoing after ~2–3 months)**  
   - Construct 4.5h sessions: 3 problems approximating (easy, medium, hard) from Shortlists or national Olympiads.  
   - Post-mortem every set:  
     - Where did you spend too long?  
     - Did you systematically exploit all constraints?  
     - Were there known “big guns” (e.g., LTE, barycentrics, extremal principle) you simply failed to reach for?

4. **Pattern mining and “library building”**  
   - Maintain a personal library (Git repo / notes) of:
     - Inequality patterns (standard transformations, substitution motifs).  
     - NT lemmas (LTE variants, multiplicative tricks, order arguments).  
     - Geometry configurations (Miquel, spiral similarity, cyclic quadrilaterals “big picture”). [anzoteh96.wordpress](https://anzoteh96.wordpress.com/maths/)
     - Combinatorial invariants, colorings, extremal templates.  
   - Tag each entry with Shortlist problems where it appears, so you can see which tools recur.

5. **Cross-referencing Shortlist years**  
   - For each year, look at the official Shortlist with solutions and see how they map to the final IMO six problems. [imo-official](https://www.imo-official.org/problems/IMO2023SL.pdf)
   - Pay attention to “near-misses”: problems that were in the Shortlist but not selected; they’re often great training at very similar difficulty.

## 5. Where to get problem streams

- **Official IMO and Shortlist PDFs** (IMO website): authoritative source for problems and official solutions. [imo-official](https://www.imo-official.org/problems/IMO2023SL.pdf)
- **AoPS problem index and forums**: filter by contest and approximate difficulty; read multiple community solutions for insight into different techniques. [artofproblemsolving](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AoPS_Wiki:Competition_ratings)
- **National Olympiad and TSTs** (USAMO, China TST, Russia, Romania, etc.): many “Shortlist-grade” problems live here; Evan Chen’s archive links to a lot of these. [web.evanchen](https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html)
- **Books like *Mathematical Olympiad Challenges* and *Problems from the Book***: curated high-difficulty collections aligned with Shortlist style. [cambomaths.wordpress](https://cambomaths.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/03/mathematical-olympiad-challenges-2nd-edition-feb-2009.pdf)
