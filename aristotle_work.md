
Aristotle is not primarily known as a mathematician in the modern sense, and he did not produce a body of original mathematical theorems like Euclid or Archimedes. However, he made several contributions to the philosophy and foundations of mathematics, and some mathematical results are attributed to him or his school. Here is a list of the main mathematical results and contributions associated with Aristotle:

**1. The Law of Excluded Middle (in logic, foundational for mathematical proof)**
- Every proposition is either true or false; there is no third truth value. This principle underpins classical mathematical reasoning (proof by contradiction, etc.).

**2. The Law of Non-Contradiction**
- A proposition cannot be both true and false at the same time in the same respect. Fundamental to mathematical consistency.

**3. Syllogistic Logic**
- Aristotle's system of syllogisms (Prior Analytics) was the first formal deductive system and remained the basis of logical reasoning in mathematics for over two millennia.

**4. The theory of definition and axiomatics**
- Aristotle articulated how mathematical definitions and axioms function (Posterior Analytics), influencing Euclid's axiomatic method.

**5. Potential vs. actual infinity**
- Aristotle distinguished between potential infinity (a process that can always be continued) and actual infinity (a completed infinite totality). He rejected actual infinity, a position that shaped mathematics until Cantor.

**6. The continuum and infinite divisibility**
- In Physics, Aristotle argued that a continuous magnitude is infinitely divisible potentially but not actually. This influenced later debates on the continuum.

**7. The wheel paradox (Aristotle's wheel paradox)**
- A paradox described in the Mechanica (traditionally attributed to Aristotle or his school): two concentric circles rolling together appear to travel different distances despite one revolution each. It stimulated later work on infinitesimals and rolling motion.

**8. The squaring of the circle (as a logical analysis)**
- Aristotle discussed the problem of squaring the circle in Prior Analytics and Sophistical Refutations, analyzing fallacious proofs — not solving it, but clarifying the logic of such problems.

**9. The distinction between arithmetic and geometry**
- Aristotle classified mathematics into arithmetic (discrete quantity) and geometry (continuous quantity), a division that structured ancient mathematics.

**10. The concept of commensurability and incommensurability**
- Aristotle discussed incommensurable magnitudes (e.g., the diagonal of a square) in Categories and Metaphysics, helping frame the Greek understanding of irrationals.

**11. The principle of the lever (Mechanica)**
- The Mechanical Problems (attributed to Aristotle's school) contains an early treatment of the lever and the law of the lever, though the rigorous proof is usually credited to Archimedes.

**12. The analysis of motion and velocity (Physics)**
- Aristotle's qualitative theory of motion (including the famous proportions relating distance, force, and resistance) was not mathematically correct by modern standards but framed later kinematic discussions.

**Important caveat:** Many works traditionally attributed to Aristotle (especially the Mechanica) are now considered pseudepigrapha, written by followers. Also, Aristotle's "mathematical results" are mostly foundational, logical, and philosophical rather than theorems in geometry or number theory. He is not credited with proving geometric theorems; his importance to mathematics lies in logic, axiomatics, and the philosophy of the infinite and continuum.
