
Here are 50 distinct **classical Euclidean-geometry problems**, mostly phrased as straightedge-and-compass constructions or proof exercises in the style of *Euclid’s Elements*. They cover foundational constructions, triangles, parallels, circles, ratios, areas, and solids. Euclid’s Book I begins with constructions such as building an equilateral triangle on a given segment, copying a segment, bisecting angles, and drawing perpendiculars; later books develop proportionality, circle geometry, areas, and solid figures. [maths.tcd](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/~dwilkins/Courses/MA232A/Euclid_ETexts/EuclidBookI_LardnerInProgress.pdf)

## Fundamental constructions

1. Construct an equilateral triangle on a given segment \(AB\).

2. Copy a given segment \(CD\) starting from a specified point \(A\).

3. Given two unequal segments, cut off from the longer one a segment equal to the shorter.

4. Bisect a given segment \(AB\).

5. Construct a line through a given point \(P\) perpendicular to a given line \(l\), where \(P\) lies on \(l\).

6. Construct a perpendicular from a point \(P\) outside a given line \(l\).

7. Bisect a given angle \(\angle ABC\).

8. Copy a given angle at a specified point on a given ray.

9. Construct an angle equal to the sum of two given angles.

10. Construct an angle equal to the difference between two given angles.

11. Draw a line through a point \(P\) parallel to a given line \(l\).

12. Divide a given segment into \(n\) equal parts.

13. Divide a segment \(AB\) internally in a prescribed ratio \(m:n\).

14. Construct the midpoint of the arc \(AB\) of a given circle.

15. Construct the perpendicular bisector of a chord of a circle.

## Triangle constructions

16. Construct a triangle given its three side lengths, provided they satisfy the triangle inequalities.

17. Construct a triangle given two sides and the included angle.

18. Construct a triangle given one side and its two adjacent angles.

19. Construct a right triangle given its hypotenuse and one leg.

20. Construct a right triangle given one leg and one acute angle.

21. Construct an isosceles triangle given its base and equal side length.

22. Construct an isosceles triangle given its base and vertex angle.

23. Construct an equilateral triangle with a given altitude.

24. Construct a triangle given its base, one base angle, and the altitude to that base.

25. Construct a triangle given its three medians.

26. Construct a triangle given its three altitudes.

27. Construct a triangle given its circumradius and its three angles.

28. Construct a triangle given its inradius and its three angles.

29. In a triangle \(ABC\), prove that the greater side subtends the greater angle.

30. In a triangle \(ABC\), prove that the greater angle lies opposite the greater side.

## Triangle centers and loci

31. Construct the circumcenter of a given triangle.

32. Construct the incenter of a given triangle.

33. Construct the centroid of a given triangle.

34. Construct the orthocenter of an acute triangle.

35. Prove that the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of a triangle are concurrent.

36. Prove that the internal angle bisectors of a triangle are concurrent.

37. Prove that the medians of a triangle are concurrent, and that the centroid divides each median in the ratio \(2:1\).

38. Prove that the three altitudes of a triangle are concurrent.

39. Find the locus of points equidistant from two fixed points \(A\) and \(B\).

40. Find the locus of points equidistant from two intersecting lines.

## Parallels, areas, and ratios

41. Prove that alternate interior angles made by a transversal with parallel lines are equal.

42. Prove the converse: if a transversal makes equal alternate interior angles with two lines, then the two lines are parallel.

43. Construct a parallelogram equal in area to a given triangle.

44. Construct a square equal in area to a given rectangle.

45. Construct a square equal in area to a given triangle.

46. Given two segments \(a\) and \(b\), construct their geometric mean \(\sqrt{ab}\).

47. Given three segments \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), construct a fourth proportional \(x\) satisfying
   \[
   a:b=c:x.
   \]

48. Prove that triangles on the same base and between the same parallels have equal area.

49. Prove that parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels have equal area.

50. Divide a given triangle into two regions of equal area by a line through a specified vertex.

## Circle geometry

A few further classical circle problems—useful if you want to replace any items above—are:

- Construct the tangent to a circle at a given point on the circle.
- Construct tangents from an external point to a given circle.
- Inscribe a circle in a given triangle.
- Circumscribe a circle about a given triangle.
- Prove that an angle in a semicircle is a right angle.
- Prove that equal chords of the same circle subtend equal angles at the center.
- Prove that equal angles at the center stand on equal chords.
- Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to \(180^\circ\).
- Construct a regular hexagon in a given circle.
- Construct a regular pentagon in a given circle.

These problems align with the classical tradition of Euclidean construction and proof, including segment bisection, angle bisection, perpendicular and parallel constructions, triangle construction from side/angle data, circle tangents, proportional segments, and equivalent-area figures.
