
A 2-isohedral tiling is a geometric pattern where the symmetry group of the tiling divides all the individual tiles into exactly two transitivity classes (or orbits). This means you need two different types of tile placement or colors to cover the plane, unlike 1-isohedral (monohedral) tilings where every tile is symmetrically identical. [1] (https://arxiv.org/html/0708.2663v1), [2] (https://mathworld.wolfram.com/IsohedralTiling.html), [3] (https://www.jaapsch.net/tilings/)

Key Concepts

• Isohedral Number (k): A measure of how many distinct tile orbits exist under the global symmetry group of the pattern, equal to 2 in this case.
• Orbital Groups: The tiles fall into two distinct groups where all tiles in the first group can be mapped to one another by symmetry operations (rotations, reflections, translations), and likewise for the second group.
• Coloring and Structure: Most 2-isohedral tilings use a 1:1 ratio of two tile types (forming a fundamental unit of two tiles), though rare variations can feature different ratios.
• Classification and Notations: Researchers classify these patterns using specialized Isohedral Tiles, Tilings, and Notations such as incidence symbols, edge labels, and adjacency rules.
