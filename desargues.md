
There are not 20 universally standardized *named* “Desargues extensions”; the literature instead contains several general frameworks with many dimension-, rank-, or configuration-specific instances. Here are 20 distinct theorem-level generalizations, extensions, converses, or structural strengthenings.

Let \(\Sigma_n\) denote \(n\)-dimensional projective space, and an \(n\)-simplex have \(n+1\) vertices.

## Simplex and higher-dimensional forms

1. **Generalized simplex Desargues theorem** — Two \(n\)-simplexes in \(\Sigma_n\), perspective from a point, are perspective from a hyperplane: intersections of corresponding subspaces lie in one fixed hyperplane.

2. **Extended Desargues theorem for all face dimensions** — If corresponding \(t\)-faces of two perspective simplexes are intersected, each intersection is a \((t-1)\)-space, for every \(t=1,\ldots,n-1\).

3. **Higher-dimensional edge-axis theorem** — The \(\binom{n+1}{2}\) intersections of corresponding edges of two perspective \(n\)-simplexes lie in a common \((n-1)\)-hyperplane.

4. **Tetrahedral Desargues theorem** — Two tetrahedra in \(\mathbf P^3\) perspective from a point have corresponding edges meeting in points that lie in a plane; corresponding triangular faces meet in lines arranged in that plane.

5. **Pentachoral Desargues theorem** — Two 4-simplexes in \(\mathbf P^4\) perspective from a point have their corresponding-edge intersections in a 3-space, with the analogous hierarchy for faces.

6. **Dual simplex Desargues theorem** — If two \(n\)-simplexes are perspective from a hyperplane, then they are perspective from a point.

7. **Strong converse for simplexes** — Under nondegeneracy assumptions, if every pair of corresponding edges of two \(n\)-simplexes meets, then all joins of corresponding vertices are concurrent.

8. **Higher-dimensional face-converse theorem** — If intersections of corresponding \(t\)-faces exhibit the prescribed \((t-1)\)-dimensional pattern in one hyperplane, then the two simplexes are centrally perspective.

9. **Semi-simplex recursive Desargues theorem** — The edge-intersection configuration of a perspective pair of simplexes itself contains a perspective pair of lower-dimensional semi-simplexes; iteration gives a nested Desargues structure.

10. **Self-replicating Desargues configuration theorem** — In the extended configuration arising from an arc, every configuration point can serve as a center of perspectivity for another appropriate pair of simplexes. The all-dimensional extension, its converse, and these configuration properties are developed explicitly in Bruen’s work. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03243.pdf)

## Generalized planar configurations

11. **Generalized Desargues theorem for triangles** — Given two triangles perspective from \(S\), construct a third triangle from intersections of selected *non-corresponding* sides. The resulting triangle is also perspective to each original triangle from \(S\). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0203022.pdf)

12. **Dual generalized Desargues theorem** — Starting with two triangles perspective from an axis \(s\), the analogous construction using cross-joins produces a third triangle perspective to both originals from \(s\). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0203022.pdf)

13. **Triangle pre-sum theorem** — The generalized construction defines a commutative “pre-sum” operation on suitable perspective triangles: \(A\boxplus B=C\), with identities such as \(A\boxplus C=B\). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0203022.pdf)

14. **Additive-group theorem for perspective triangles** — After adding formal degenerate/infinite triangles, the induced sum of triangles becomes an Abelian group isomorphic, in barycentric coordinates, to \((\mathbb R^3,+)\). This is a substantial algebraic strengthening of the ordinary incidence result. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0203022.pdf)

15. **Generalized Desargues theorem for complete \(n\)-points** — A generalization from triangles and complete quadrangles to projective complete \(n\)-point configurations, formulated inductively in terms of generalized harmonic-point structures. [mdpi](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/9/9/1018)

16. **Generalized converse Desargues theorem for complete \(n\)-points** — The converse incidence condition for those complete \(n\)-point configurations; the cited work identifies ordinary converse Desargues and perspective quadrangles as low-rank special cases. [mdpi](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/9/9/1018)

17. **Perspective quadrangles theorem** — The quadrangle-level special case of the complete-\(n\)-point framework: appropriately matched quadrangles satisfying the generalized axial condition are centrally perspective. [mdpi](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/9/9/1018)

18. **Arbitrary finite point-set / generalized-line Desargues theorem** — In \(\mathbf P^3\), the triangle requirement can be relaxed to suitable finite point sets on two planes, allowing coincident corresponding points and collinear triples; generalized lines and “triangle-connected” sets replace the nondegenerate-triangle hypotheses. [onlinelibrary.wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/276108)

## Algebraic and involutory extensions

19. **Determinantal Pappus–Desargues theorem** — A vector/determinant identity in a 3-dimensional vector space specializes to Desargues when the relevant concurrency determinant vanishes, but remains valid without that special degeneracy. Thus it is a strict algebraic generalization. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.12455.pdf)

20. **Generalized Desargues involution theorem for pencils of quadrics** — In projective space of arbitrary dimension over a field of characteristic not \(2\), a pencil of quadrics induces conjugate point-pairs—an involution—on a line precisely under stated nondegeneracy conditions. It generalizes the classical Desargues involution theorem; the butterfly theorem is a special case of the classical involution result. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12200.pdf)

The cleanest unifying statement is item 2: ordinary Desargues is the \(n=2\), \(t=1\) case of the simplex theorem.
