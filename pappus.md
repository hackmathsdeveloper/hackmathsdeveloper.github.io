
Pappus of Alexandria’s principal mathematical work is the *Mathematical Collection* (*Synagoge*), an eight-book compilation covering geometry, arithmetic, astronomy, and mechanics. His best-known results include the hexagon theorem, the centroid theorems, an area theorem generalizing Pythagoras, and results about chains of tangent circles. However, **attribution** matters: the *Collection* combines Pappus’s own contributions with results, proofs, and problems inherited from earlier mathematicians. It is not a catalogue of discoveries all made by him. [britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Pappus-of-Alexandria)

Below is a broad catalogue of the principal results and subjects in his mathematical works—not a proposition-by-proposition inventory. A literally complete list of everything he wrote is impossible because Book I and part of Book II of the *Collection*, as well as other writings, are lost. [crossworks.holycross](https://crossworks.holycross.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1062&context=hc_books)

## 1. The principal results associated with Pappus

### 1. Pappus’s hexagon theorem

Take three points \(A,B,C\) on one line and three points \(a,b,c\) on another. Define
\[
X=Ab\cap aB,\qquad
Y=Ac\cap aC,\qquad
Z=Bc\cap bC.
\]
Then \(X,Y,Z\) are collinear, provided the intersections are interpreted appropriately. This result appears among the lemmas associated with Euclid’s *Porisms* in Book VII and is now a foundational theorem of projective geometry. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria)

### 2. Pappus–Guldinus centroid theorems

These are two distinct results recorded in Book VII:

- Surface area: when a plane curve of length \(L\) revolves about an external axis in its plane, the generated surface has area
  \[
  S=2\pi \bar r L,
  \]
  where \(\bar r\) is the distance of the curve’s centroid from the axis.
- Volume: when a plane region of area \(A\) revolves about an external axis in its plane, the generated solid has volume
  \[
  V=2\pi \bar r A,
  \]
  where \(\bar r\) is now the distance of the region’s area centroid from the axis.

In each case, the measure of the generating object is multiplied by the distance travelled by its centroid. The theorems were later rediscovered by Paul Guldin. [britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Pappus-of-Alexandria)

### 3. Pappus’s area theorem

Book IV begins with a generalization of the Pythagorean theorem: suitably constructed parallelograms on the sides of an arbitrary triangle satisfy an area-addition relation. Unlike Pythagoras’s theorem, the triangle need not be right-angled and the figures need not be squares; the construction determines the required parallelogram on the third side. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria)

### 4. Tangent-circle results and the Pappus chain

Book IV studies circles tangent to one another, including circles inscribed in an *arbelos*—the region between three semicircles constructed on a segment and its two subsegments. This is the setting for the tangent-circle configuration now called the Pappus chain. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria)

### 5. Focus–directrix characterization of conics

Book VII gives a locus result of the form
\[
\frac{PF}{d(P,\ell)}=e,
\]
where \(F\) is a fixed point, \(\ell\) a fixed line, and \(e>0\) a constant. The locus is:

- an ellipse when \(0<e<1\);
- a parabola when \(e=1\);
- a hyperbola when \(e>1\).

The *Collection* preserves early proofs of these focus–directrix properties. This should not be confused with a claim that Pappus invented conic sections. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria)

### 6. Pappus’s locus problem

Given several fixed lines, find the locus of a point whose distances—or segments drawn at specified angles—to those lines satisfy a prescribed product-ratio condition. For four lines, a modern formulation is
\[
d_1d_2=k\,d_3d_4.
\]
Pappus discusses this family of problems in Book VII. Its later investigation helped stimulate the development of Cartesian analytic geometry. It is a problem family rather than one isolated theorem. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria)

### 7. A centroid-preserving construction in a triangle

Let \(H,\Theta,K\) lie on the successive sides of triangle \(ABC\), with
\[
\frac{AH}{HB}
=
\frac{B\Theta}{\Theta C}
=
\frac{CK}{KA}.
\]
Then triangles \(ABC\) and \(H\Theta K\) have the same centroid. This is Theorem 1 in the cited translation of Book VIII. [crossworks.holycross](https://crossworks.holycross.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1061&context=hc_books)

### 8. Constructions concerning ellipses

Book VIII includes:

- a construction of an ellipse through five given points, in the circumstances treated there;
- a construction of an ellipse’s axes from a pair of conjugate diameters.

These appear as Propositions 14 and 15 in the numbering summarized by the historical account. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria)

## 2. The *Mathematical Collection*, book by book

The following table gives the broader inventory, including material Pappus preserved from his predecessors.

| Book | Main results, problems, and mathematical content |
|---|---|
| I | Lost. Its subject is uncertain, although arithmetic has been suggested.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| II | Large-number arithmetic and a multiplication procedure associated with a lost work of Apollonius; calculations involving the numerical values of Greek letters. Only part survives.  [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter/549685160?guestAccessKey=) |
| III | Finding two mean proportionals; duplication of the cube and related cube-scaling problems; arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic means; a classification of ten kinds of means; constructions involving the five regular polyhedra inscribed in a sphere.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| IV | The area theorem; circle tangencies and arbelos problems; Archimedes’s spiral; Nicomedes’s conchoid; the quadratrix; a spherical helix and the area of a spherical region bounded by it; angle trisection and related constructions.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| V | Isoperimetric comparisons—areas for a fixed perimeter and volumes for a fixed surface area; comparisons of regular solids; descriptions of the thirteen Archimedean solids; the surface area and volume of the sphere. Much of this develops earlier work, including that of Zenodorus and Archimedes.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| VI | Mathematical astronomy, spherical geometry, and optics: commentary on works by Theodosius, Autolycus, Aristarchus, and Euclid, collectively associated with the “Little Astronomy.”  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| VII | Analysis and synthesis; Pappus’s locus problem; centroid theorems; the hexagon theorem; conics; lemmas related to involution, harmonic relations, and poles and polars; accounts of earlier works on advanced geometry.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| VIII | Mechanics, centers of gravity, and mechanical powers, interspersed with pure geometry, including the triangle-centroid result and ellipse constructions.  [crossworks.holycross](https://crossworks.holycross.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1061&context=hc_books) |

### Two important connections within this material

The two-mean-proportionals problem in Book III asks for \(x,y\) such that
\[
a:x=x:y=y:b.
\]
It connects geometric construction to cube scaling; Pappus presents multiple approaches, including an instrumental construction. It is therefore important not to mistake these constructions for unrestricted straightedge-and-compass solutions. [crossworks.holycross](https://crossworks.holycross.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1062&context=hc_books)

Book VII’s “Treasury of Analysis” serves a different purpose: it preserves descriptions and supporting lemmas for advanced works by Euclid, Apollonius, Aristaeus, and Eratosthenes, many now lost. Its value is both mathematical and historical—it lets us reconstruct portions of a much larger Greek geometrical tradition. [britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Pappus-of-Alexandria)

## 3. His other mathematical writings

Besides the *Collection*, Pappus wrote the following mathematical or closely related commentaries:

| Work | Subject and survival |
|---|---|
| Commentary on Euclid’s *Elements*, especially Book X | Irrational magnitudes. The commentary on Book X survives through an Arabic manuscript; other material is fragmentary.  [crossworks.holycross](https://crossworks.holycross.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1062&context=hc_books) |
| Commentary on Ptolemy’s *Almagest* | Mathematical astronomy. The portion concerning Books V and VI survives.  [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter/549685160?guestAccessKey=) |
| Commentary on Diodorus’s *Analemma* | A further commentary mentioned by Pappus himself.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |
| Commentary on Ptolemy’s *Harmonics* | Mathematical music theory; listed among his other writings.  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria) |

Thus, a list of “Pappus’s results” should distinguish three things: the named theorems associated with him, the wider mathematics preserved in the *Collection*, and his explanatory work on earlier mathematical texts. Treating all three as original discoveries would substantially overstate what the surviving evidence establishes. [crossworks.holycross](https://crossworks.holycross.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1062&context=hc_books)
